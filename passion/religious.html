Jesus is the Christ

Jesus Christ is the center of my faith. I believe He is the Eternal Son of the Father — that He lived, taught, suffered, died, and rose again, and that His infinite Atonement heals, saves, and changes us. Everything else I teach or write about faith is an appendage to that message.

If you want my own voice on scripture, covenants, and discipleship, start with my Talks. If you want official teaching from The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, go to ChurchofJesusChrist.org.

Who I am

I am a member of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. I try to live a Christ-centered life — repentance when I fall short, covenants that bind me to Him, and ordinary discipleship at home and in my congregation. I like teaching and giving talks; the Plan of Salvation and the Atonement of Jesus Christ are themes I return to again and again.

Mission — Mérida Mexico Mission

From May 18, 2005 to May 18, 2007 I served as a full-time missionary in the Mérida Mexico Mission, on the Yucatán Peninsula. I learned Yucatec Maya first, then Spanish — languages that live side by side in many homes and plazas across the peninsula.

Much of my time was away from the Caribbean tourist corridor. Mérida sits inland, and I also served in smaller interior communities in Yucatán state — places like Tzucacab, a southern municipio roughly 145 kilometers from Mérida, near towns such as Peto and Tekax. Life there is agricultural and community-centered: plaza, family compounds, milpa and pasture, tropical heat, and a limestone country without rivers, where wells and cenotes are how water is found.

Everyday Yucatec food is built on tortillas and beans, brightened by sour orange and achiote — cochinita pibil, panuchos and salbutes, Monday frijol con puerco, evening marquesitas in the plaza, and tropical fruit in the heat. Those years taught me to love people, to listen across language and culture, and to invite others to come unto Christ.

Questions you might have

Short answers in my own words, pointing to official Church pages. I am not an official spokesman for The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.

Who is Jesus Christ — and what did He do for us?

Jesus Christ is the Son of God, our Savior and Redeemer. He lived a perfect life, taught His gospel, suffered and died for our sins, and rose again. Because of His Atonement — His suffering in Gethsemane and on the cross, and His Resurrection — we can be forgiven as we repent, healed, and live again. That is the heart of my faith.

Learn more: The Savior, Jesus Christ

Are Latter-day Saints Christians?

Yes. Members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints believe Jesus Christ is the Son of God and the Savior of the world, and we strive to follow Him. The Church teaches that it is a restoration of the Church Jesus established — Christian, though not Catholic or Protestant. Where our beliefs differ from other Christians, I point you to the Church’s own explanations rather than argue them here.

Learn more: Common questions · Newsroom FAQ

What is the purpose of life — and what happens after we die?

We believe we lived with God as spirits before birth; earth life is a time to learn, grow, and choose; and after death our spirits continue. Because of Jesus Christ, all will be resurrected. His grace helps us repent and become more like Him, with hope of living with God and our families forever. Death is not the end.

Learn more: God’s plan of happiness

Do you believe in the Bible? What is the Book of Mormon?

Yes — we revere the Bible as the word of God. The Book of Mormon is another testament of Jesus Christ: a scriptural record that testifies of Him and, together with the Bible, witnesses of God’s plan. It is meant to reinforce and expand our witness of Christ, not replace the Bible.

Learn more: The Bible and the Book of Mormon

Would I be welcome at church? What are Sundays like?

Yes — everyone is invited. Sunday worship usually includes sacrament meeting (hymns, prayers, short messages by members, and the sacrament — bread and water in remembrance of Jesus’s sacrifice) and a second hour of classes. You don’t have to speak, donate, or already believe everything to visit.
